Echtra midst the Sidhe

Chapter 1:<br> Samhain Festival

Look to the horizon and despair. My master calls, and the Wild Hunt answers. Tonight, I take one life. Soon, I shall return… and the Hunt shall take them all.

Dullahan

The campaign starts on the last day of the Old Year and the beginning of New - at Samhain Festival in Cúilfeorna, a northenmost permanent settlement in Irgaelia. The times were relativelly peaceful, the weather kind, and the crops plenty - but on this fatefull day, woe hangs above the people of Irgaelia.

Dullahan, the Headless Horseman, secretly sent by The Morrigan - a goddess of war, fate and death - is on his way to Cúilfeorna and neither gate nor door will bar him from taking a life tonight, and with the death of his victim, the beginning of the Year of Woe will be marked.

The heroes' objective in this chapter is not clear that the beginning: the arrival of Dullahan cannot be predicted. Let them get to know eachother, particpate in the festivities, feasts and fortune-telling, before the Old Year dies, but make sure to inform the players that the midnight ritual of lighting the pyre is important: they must take the fire and bring it home to protect the dwelling from the Sidhe in the coming year. Besides, the prospect of feasting, dancing and telling tales should keep them active until late night hours.

It had been a good year - more than a few good years in fact.

You have gathered in Cúilfeorna, with the Tribe of the Cold Shore (Tuath na Trá Fuara) to celebrate it's end, and the beginning of - hopefully - the next of the plentiful years. Even though the sun had yet not fallen behind the horizon, the festivities have already begun.

Children play in guises of the Gods and the Aos Si, while men and women eat, drink and dance around the great pyre, that is to be lit before midnight by the Druid.

It is a time for songs and feasts, and yet, you feel deep unease: it is a night of ghosts, after all, when the Otherworld seeps into the land of the living.

Cúilfeorna

The settlement of Cúilfeorna is a trading community, a bit secluded due to the cold harbor,and far-off to the north enough so that the other Tuath do not interfere with the hearty people of Trá Fuara. The settlement survives largely by the profits it makes supplying the ships of daring adventurers who seek to sail north through the Frozen Sea, in search of mythical lands, and awaiting fortunes - and even then, it' in decline from it's past glory, when it was a bastion in the north against the fearsome Fomorians.

The settlement is surrounded by small farms, and large pastures.

Climate and Weather

Though the north is colder than elsewhere in Irgaelia, the winter is yet to come - but the first snow is expected to fall any time now. Special clothing protecting against the cold weather is not required yet at this stage of the campaign.

Denizens of Cúilfeorna

The people of Cúilfeorna are the Tribe of the Cold Shore — Tuath na Trá Fuara. Consisting not only of locals, but also of many misfits from other tribes who sought refuge in this secluded land, the tribe is a true cultural melting pot, with humans, elves, dwarves, gnomes, and halflings constituting most of its members. However, half-orcs and even tieflings are welcome here as long as they respect the traditions. There are no dragonborn in Cúilfeorna—more due to the rarity of this race than any antipathies.

Other Tribes

At this time, many people of all tribes have arrived to Cúilfeorna to attend this years Samhain Festival there, due to the pressence of Archdruid of the North - the oldest of the Druids, who will be responsible for lighting the fire.

The Trá Fuara, are welcoming to these visitors, and invite them to the festivities.

Archdruid of the North

Keegan mac Éamoinn is the oldest of the Druids (SRD p.351) in Irgaelia, and in his twilight years had made his abode in Cúilfeorna - "the cold preserves", as he says.

Not only is he a guardian of nature, but also a prolific seer, that had garnered respect from the people of Irgaelia for his wisdom and insight.

As the party is awaiting midnight, for the ritual of Lighting the Pyre to be conducted, encourage them to seek Keegan out - before he has to tend to his duties, the old elf is mingling with the crowd, but should be easy to find- and his prophecies always come true, or at least all the people claim so.

During the festival, the Archdruid is in a sorrowful mood, as he had predicted that this is his last Samhain: but he doesn't know what exactly will transpire, or that he will die on this very evening.

Keegan is the only person present in Cúilfeorna who could properly bless the fire that would protect the land and the people from the cold during the following year. Unfortunatelly, his fate is sealed: Keegan mac Éamoinn dies tonight.

Important NPCs

  • Fionn Halfhand, the chieftan of Trá Fuara, is a stout human who had lost three fingers in his left hand to cold during one of his expeditions (táin). He is usually overtly grumpy, but has a heart of gold.
  • Keegan mac Éamoinn, an elderly elf, and the current Archdruid of the North.

Random Encounters

During the party’s time in Cúilfeorna, these encounters are designed to subtly foreshadow the challenges they will face throughout the campaign. The players should experience at least some of these encounters before the midnight hour arrives and Samhain reaches its peak with the lighting of the sacred pyre.

These moments serve to introduce the players to the lore, mood, and themes of the land without revealing any of the major twists ahead. Each encounter offers a glimpse into the threats looming over Irgaelia, while also immersing the party in its rich cultural tapestry.

Feel free to run as many of the encounters as you see fit, depending on how engaged your players are. When you feel the party is sufficiently familiar with the tone of the campaign and its underlying mysteries, you may transition to the Lighting the Pyre section, marking the official start of the adventure.

Samhain Rites

After the sunset, the Samhain rites can begin. Encourage the party to participate in various activities with the locals.

As the sun dips below the horizon and the chill of the evening sets in, the village of Cúilfeorna transforms in preparation for the Samhain festivities. Bonfires crackle at the settlement edges, casting long, dancing shadows, while villagers don costumes representing the spirits, gods, and monsters — an ancient tradition meant to confuse any malicious spirits that might wander into the world on this night.

Stalls line the central square, offering baked goods, carved turnips, and hearty stews. The air is filled with the smell of spices and smoke, and the sounds of music and laughter blend with the whisper of leaves stirred by an unearthly wind. Yet beneath the merriment lies a palpable tension — everyone knows Samhain is not just a time for celebration, but a time when the veil between worlds thins, and dangers unseen may slip through.

Feast of the Ancestors

During the day, long tables and benches made of spruce logs are put outside the settlements, for all to feast together, in memory of the ancestors. None shall go hungry, while the feast lasts, and none shall be rejected from the common table.

Long tables of split spruce wood, raw and fragrant, stretch across a field just beyond the settlement, illuminated by the soft glow of torches and bonfires. The earth beneath is still firm from the late harvest, but a chill lingers as the wind whispers through the nearby forest.

:::::::::::::::::::::::::

During the feast, tales of might and valor, as well as those of clever exploits are told. Encourage the players to participate. You can ask:

  • How did their fian (party) meet?
  • Which tuath (tribe) do they belong to?
  • Have they ever encountered a Seelie?

The characters that participate in the feast and tell a story, succeeding a DC 12 Performance or DC 14 Charisma check benefit from the Heroes' Feast spell. You can forgo this check if the player decides to actually tell a story, encouraging your table to immerse themselves with their charcters.

Additionally, at the end of the feast, the best story will be voted on. If any of the characters have surpassed DC 16 on their check, or roleplayed through the storytelling, determine a winner by picking the highest score.

Campaign Tracker

The character who won is granted Inspiration, and a title of Samhain Storyteller - mark this on the Campaign Tracker.

Story of Lorcán and Aisling

Long ago, there was a lord who ruled these very lands, a man named Lorcán, and his heart was as dark as the shadows beneath the ancient oaks he called home. He had a wife, a fair lady of the village, Aisling by name, who was as kind as he was cruel. She had a smile that could turn the coldest winter day into spring, and a laugh that brought joy to all who heard it. She was beloved by all who knew her... and this is where the trouble began.

Ye see, Lorcán, was a man who could not bear to see what he loved out of his grasp. He grew envious of the joy Aisling spread to the villagers, envious of the way their eyes would light up when they saw her, envious of the love they bore her that he could never truly own.

One Samhain night, Lorcán's envy turned to rage. He accused his wife of betraying him, of giving her love to others, though it was naught but his own twisted jealousy that conjured such thoughts. In a fit of madness, he took her deep into the heart of the forest, to a place where no light could reach, a place said to be touched by the Otherworld itself.

With a heart blackened by his own envy, Lorcán made a pact with the dark spirits that roam the wilds on this night of all nights. He bound Aisling to the earth with roots and vines, turning her into a great oak tree, her branches forever reaching out as if begging for mercy. The spirits granted his wish, but they are tricksters, as ye all know.

The spirits told Lorcán that as long as his wife remained bound to the forest, so too would he. And so, the Green Lord was cursed to wander the woods, never able to leave the shade of the trees that he once loved, nor touch the sunlight that would warm his cold, twisted heart. He became a spirit himself, a twisted wraith that haunts these woods to this very day, jealous of every soul who dares to be happy under the light of the moon.

If you wish to set the tone, or give the players more time to prepare a story to tell, you can read the Story of Lorcán and Aisling, which foreshadows future events in the campaign.

Costume Procession

The annual Costume Procession is a grand parade where revelers from all corners of the region come to showcase their creativity and honor the spirits of Samhain. The procession starts at dusk, and climbs up the hill of the Elder Oak.

The Elder Oak of Cúilfeorna

is one of Four Sacred Trees in Irgaelia, bearing great religious significance. The tree is one of the main reasons why this community clings to the hostile and cold north.

When the procession begins, read the following fragment:

As the procession starts, the sounds of music fill the air, joining with the laughter of revelers as they march through the streets. The flickering glow of lanterns casts dancing shadows on the cobblestones beneath your feet. The crowd is a swirl of faces, some familiar and others hidden behind elaborate masks. A sense of both celebration and unease lingers in the air, as if the night holds something deeper—something more dangerous—beneath the surface.

The procession begins to gather speed, the rhythm of the dancers growing more frenetic as the streets narrow. As the crowd moves forward, you catch glimpses of the strange figures lining the route: some are watching with keen interest, others with an air of sadness or anticipation. The sound of footsteps, music, and whispered conversations creates an overwhelming sense of being swept up into something much larger than yourself.

The procession itself is a great opportunity for the party to stumble upon some of the encounters prepared for Cúilfeorna. During the chaotic passage up the hill overlooking the settlement, the party might also meet Fionn Halfhand - the chieftain of the Trá Fuara.

Fionn Halfhand

The interaction with Fionn is not particularly important at this stage of the campaign, but Fionn might become a more important character during its course, therefore take notes on how the party interacted with him.

If the interaction was particularly unpleasant for any reason, consider replacing Fionn with an acolyte of Keegan as the person sending the party to gather the druids in Comarile at the end of this chapter, after the departure of Dullahan. Nonetheless, keep track of Fionns impression of the party, as he may appear from act 2 onwards.

The reason that Fionn recognizes the party is that they are arrivals from other clans, and the chieftan of Trá Fuara wishes to hear the news.

After you decide that you have ran enough of the encounters during this section, the crowd arrives at the top of the hill. You can read the following fragment:

Finally, the procession halts at the foot of the Elder Oak. A figure steps forward, their face hidden behind a bone mask, and begins to speak in a booming voice:

“This night, we celebrate the spirits of our ancestors, and honor those who have gone before us. As the veil between the worlds thins, we remember... and we watch.”

The figure is Keegan mac Éamoinn, The Archdruid of the North himself, leading the procession uphil, where an effigy of The Autumn King will be hanged on the Elder Oak branches, as is his annual duty. Thus begins the Hanging of the Sidhe.

Hanging of the Sidhe

As the procession crests the hill, the hanging of the Autumn King’s effigy on the Sacred Oak marks the changing season and the warding of unseelie fae.

The effigy is a striking figure of woven branches, adorned with amber leaves, dried berries, and wheat stalks. Its ash-wood face bears a crooked smile, its neck adorned with a frost-touched holly necklace and silver charms. A crown of painted white antlers rests atop its head, evoking the coming snows.

Villagers carry it forward with reverence. Children whisper in awe, and the air grows colder, as if winter’s breath lingers on the horizon.

An ill omen marks this year’s Samhain: the first snow falls before the King is hanged. The elders murmur among themselves, believing the early snowfall signals an especially brutal winter ahead.

\column

As the effigy is hoisted on a hempen rope looped over one of the Elder Oak’s branches, ask the present characters to make a DC 13 Perception check. On a success, a character notices the effigy’s eyes gleam with an amber light for an instant—just before the second ill omen strikes. The rope suddenly snaps, and the effigy of the Autumn King crashes to the ground.

As the villagers recover from the shock of the broken effigy, a lone crow alights upon the Elder Oak, cawing loudly three times before flying away. The bird leaves behind a single black feather that drifts down and lands on the heart of the shattered effigy.

Each of these omens alone would weigh heavily on the hearts of the villagers, but for three to occur in a single night—it cannot be mere coincidence. Murmurs of unease slowly swell into a tide of panic.

Ill Omens

This year’s Samhain is rife with ill omens, serving as foreshadowing for the larger campaign. While the omens in this chapter are designed to build tension and hint at events to come, be mindful of your party’s engagement. Adjust the level of foreshadowing based on how much intrigue and mystery your players respond to, ensuring the narrative remains engaging without overwhelming them.

The Sleepy Tressym

As the procession winds its way toward the hill of the Elder Oak, torches and lanterns casting warm pools of light against the gathering darkness, a strange sight catches the party's attention. Deep within the forest to the east, faint blue lights flicker and dance among the trees.

The lights pulse gently, like heartbeats of captured starlight, weaving patterns through the darkened boughs. They seem to beckon, a silent invitation away from the procession's ordered chaos. The sound of laughter and music fades slightly as you focus on the glade, and for a moment, the forest feels closer, more present, as if the veil between worlds grows thinner still.

If the party investigates, they find a small clearing just off the processional route. The glade is carpeted with moss that glows faintly in the twilight, and butterflies of pure blue light flit lazily through the air, leaving trails of luminescence in their wake.

At the center of the glade, a small winged cat bats playfully at the light-butterflies with velvet paws. Her fur is the color of storm clouds touched by moonlight, and tiny feathered wings fold against her sides. She rolls onto her back, catching a butterfly between her paws, but her eyes droop heavily, as if the effort of play exhausts her greatly. When she notices you, she sits up, yawns widely enough to show needle-sharp teeth, and mews softly.

The tressym, Niamh, is extraordinarily sleepy and appears quite young, perhaps even a kitten. She shows no fear of the party, only curiosity tinged with drowsiness. Interacting with Niamh If any character offers her food, attention, or—most appealingly—a warm shoulder or lap to sleep upon, Niamh immediately takes them up on the offer. She purrs contentedly, a sound like distant thunder softened by cotton, and the blue butterflies slowly fade from view as she settles. A successful DC 12 Arcana or Nature check reveals that tressym are rare fey-touched creatures, known for their intelligence and their ability to detect poison. The blue butterflies, however, are unusual—manifestations of dream-magic, perhaps, or glamour from the Otherworld.

Niamh will become a companion to whichever character shows her the most kindness. She is content to sleep in pouches, on shoulders, or in arms, waking occasionally to bat at interesting things or demand treats. She seems utterly harmless—a drowsy, playful creature of simple wants. However, Niamh carries a secret she herself does not fully remember. She was once the beloved companion of the Spring Princess of the Seelie Court, but became lost during one of the Court's wanderings through the mortal realm. The Morrigan discovered her, and in her cruelty, placed a subtle enchantment upon the little tressym—not to control her, but to see through her eyes, to hear through her ears, and to know what the heroes would do. For now, this matters little. Niamh is genuinely affectionate and truly does want nothing more than treats, naps, and play. But the goddess watches, patient as the raven on the branch, waiting for the moment when knowledge becomes power.

If the party chooses to bring Niamh with them, she falls asleep almost immediately, occasionally twitching her wings or making soft chittering noises in her dreams.

Lighting the Pyres

The most important, and last of the Samhain Rites is the lighting of the pyres around the hill of the Elder Oak.

The crowd gathers in a great circle around the unlit pyres, their breath misting in the cold night air. Above, the stars wheel in their ancient patterns, and the Elder Oak stands dark against the sky, its branches reaching like gnarled fingers toward the heavens. Keegan mac Éamoinn steps forward, his bone mask now removed to reveal an aged elven face marked by sorrow and acceptance. In his hands, he carries a torch blessed by the old rituals, its flame dancing with colors that seem too bright, too pure, for ordinary fire.

Keegan moves from pyre to pyre with deliberate slowness, speaking words in the Old Tongue that few present understand but all feel in their bones. As each pyre ignites, the flames leap high, casting the Elder Oak in stark relief. The heat pushes back against the cold, and for a moment, it feels as though the darkness itself retreats.

"The fire protects," Keegan intones, his voice carrying across the hilltop. "The fire remembers. The fire endures. Let each household carry the sacred flame home, that the warmth of the ancestors might guard against the long night and the wandering spirits. Let none go into the dark unprotected, for on this night of all nights, the Otherworld draws near."

The crowd murmurs in response, a sound like wind through distant trees. Families begin to approach with unlit torches, candles, and lanterns, ready to carry home the blessed flame.

Arrival of Dullahan

As the bonfire burns, the party may see a figure approaching ahorse. Dullahan arrives to put the first piece of the prophecy into motion.

The crowd freezes in fear as the rider in otherworldly armor proclaims the coming of the Wild Hunt from his severed head, held high above. You can use the prepared speech or use your own.

As the last words of Dullahan's prophecy ring in the air, he and six Lesser Fae Hounds that form near him spring into action, and run towards Keegan.

During this encounter, it is critically important, that Keegan mac Éamoinn dies, but no other life is taken. As there is noone other than the party that has the tools needed to harm the headless horseman, he should spring right at the Archdruid, ignoring other creatures; in an unlikely event that he would need to attack other creatures, he strikes to incapacitate, not kill.

The Hounds on the other hand, have a different role to play out during this encounter: to disperse the crowd, draw attention, and knock the party aside. Their attacks should also be aimed to incapacitate rather, than kill. Instead of attacking, they may throw themselves between Dullahan and the party, attempt to knock the party prone, or even use a grappling attack, in an attempt to immobilize the adventurers.

Once Keegan is defeated, Dullahan laughs, and begins to ride away. The Fae Hounds use their Ethereal Step ability to follow their master.

Aftermath

With Keegan mac Éamoinn dead, and the prophecy of Dullahan looming over the people of Cúilfeorna, the characters - as the only men and women there that would be ready for the road - are asked by Fionn Halfhand to travel to Comairle, carry the grim prohecy to the town of gathering and seek guidance from the archdruids, as he cannot leave Cúilfeorna without a leader.

Thus begins their adventure, and the effort to halt the end of the world as they know it.

Hear me, mortals of fleeting breath… I am the harbinger, the herald of what stirs in the shadows beyond your feeble sight. The time has come, as it has been written in blood and bone. No flame, no circle of stones, no druidic charm will keep death from your door."

“On this night, when the veil is thin, I ride not alone. The Hunt awakens. The Hounds of the Otherworld howl for flesh, the riders thirst for your souls. None shall escape their grasp—man, woman, or child. The Hunt shall sweep this land clean, like fire through autumn leaves."

“You cling to life, but your names are already whispered in the darkness. Morrigan sharpens her talons. I am but the first shadow. When the full darkness comes, the Wild Hunt will ride under skies black with storm and death. And you will be nothing more than dust and memory, swept away in its wake.”

“Look to the horizon and despair. The master calls, and the Wild Hunt answers. Tonight, I take one life. In a year, I shall return… and the Hunt shall take them all.
