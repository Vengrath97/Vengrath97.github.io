Road to Comairle

The road to Comairle leads south from Cúilfeorna, through the Oldwood and Ravenpass, and in normal conditions would take a week.

Each of these areas propose a challenge before the adventurers that can be resolved in more than one way; but as the party is sent with an urgent task, they may as well ignore them, instead pressing on to reach Comairle as soon as possible.

By the time the heroes get to the Oldwood, it is already very cold. Blizzards rage across the land, covering the lands with a thick blanket of snow.

Effects of Cold Weather

Creatures exposed to the cold must succeed on a DC 10 Constitution saving throw at the end of each hour or gain one level of exhaustion. Creatures with resistance or immunity to cold damage automatically succeed on the saving throw, as do creatures wearing cold weather gear (thick coats, gloves, and the like) and creatures naturally adapted to cold climates.

On Random Encounters: Their Place in This Campaign

This campaign is designed to deliver a focused and streamlined narrative, emphasizing impactful scenes and meaningful encounters. While random encounters have their place in emergent, sandbox-style adventures, this campaign is structured to provide a cohesive story with intentional pacing.

Why Minimize Random Encounters? The design philosophy behind this campaign prioritizes:

  • Narrative Momentum: Maintaining a sense of progression without unnecessary diversions.
  • Meaningful Choices: Allowing the party's actions and decisions to remain at the forefront.
  • Immersive Storytelling: Ensuring that every encounter contributes to the overarching narrative or character development.

However, it’s understood that preparing for each session can sometimes be a challenge. There are moments when random encounters can serve as a useful tool, whether to:

  • Provide a breather for the DM to gather thoughts or adapt to unexpected player choices.
  • Fill time due to player absences or scheduling issues.
  • Offer additional combat for groups that particularly enjoy tactical challenges.

When and How to Use Random Encounters If you choose to include random encounters, consider tailoring them to your group’s preferences and the campaign's tone. To integrate them seamlessly:

  • Tie Them to the Setting: Encounters should reflect the environment, lore, or themes of the current chapter.
  • Add Narrative Relevance: Use encounters as a vehicle to foreshadow events, introduce NPCs, or reveal hints about the story.
  • Focus on Player Experience: Ensure they are engaging, challenging, and offer opportunities for creativity or character growth.

Tools for DMs To support you in these moments, an appendix of random encounters has been included. Use them sparingly and adapt them as needed. They’re designed as a flexible resource rather than a core component of the campaign.

Oldwood

Oldwood is not without its perils. Strange beasts, some thought long extinct or never seen beyond the bounds of myth, roam its depths. Travelers report the eerie calls of shadow harts—black, spectral stags said to foretell death—or glimpses of moss-limbed giants that vanish when pursued.

Oldwood Hunting

There are many animals that can be hunted in the Old Wood, but trying to do so may gather the attention of Old Bran (see "Druids of the Oldwood" section below). Nonetheless, with a successful DC 12 Survival check, the characters can track down a pack of animals in about an hour.

Druids of the Oldwood

The forest is also home to mortal inhabitants, most notably the Garran, a reclusive druidic sect who claim to have lived in harmony with nature since time immemorial. The Garran are known for their deep understanding of nature and their enigmatic prophecies, which often draw the desperate and the curious to their hidden enclaves.

The Garran of the Oldwood are a small group of shapeshifting druids living in tree hollows and caves, spending most of their lives in animal forms. Their leader, Old Bran is a Garran Druid whose chosen form is that of a Giant Squirrel. Old Bran notices the party when they enter the forest, and decides to observe the visitors to gauge if they may be appropriate group to help them in their struggle, and reveals himself only when he believes he will be able to garner help from the adventurers, for example:

  • When a party member gains third level of exhaustion as a result of the cold weather.
  • When a party tries to track animals to hunt and make clothing out of.
  • When the party expresses concern about the state of the Oldwood.

The other Garran are unwilling to cooperate with outsiders in their human forms, but may help them during their tasks in the forest in their animal forms. If the party agrees to help, Old Bran passes them the knowledge about the Frostroot (see "The Frostbound Blight"), and gives them direction towards the Hearttree.

From this point on, if a hunt or a random encouter turns sour for the adventurers, a Garran Druid in animal form may arrive and help them, either by distracting enemies as a small rodent, or chase them off as a powerful bear.

The Frostbound Blight

The supernatural winter has awakened an ancient blight buried deep beneath the Oldwood — an entity of the Otherworld known as the Frostroot. This parasitic growth spreads an icy corruption through the roots of the forest, freezing the lifeblood of the trees and twisting creatures into frostbitten monstrosities.

\column The druids have located a central node of the Frostroot’s power—a frost-coated hollow beneath an ancient oak known as the Hearttree. However, they cannot act: their traditions forbid them from taking violent action against the forest, even if corrupted. Cutting down the Hearttree or burning the Frostroot would be a betrayal of their sacred oaths. Thus, they turn to the adventurers, outsiders who are not bound by these sacred restrictions, to solve the problem for them.

Frostroot

The Frostroot is an elemental spirit from the Otherworld, which found his home in the Oldwood centuries ago. Only recently, his connection to nature has enabled - or rather cursed - him to hear the beating of the Heart of Winter in the land of everwinter. As a result, the spirit went seemingly irreversibly insane, and began to unwittingly transform the trees and animals into warped, frozen horrors.

Even if his madness had been restored by a use of Greater Restoration or similar spell, it would be soon for naught, for his connection with the Heat of Winter is profound, and would quickly overwhelm the spirit again.

The form of the Frostroot is that of a giant earthworm with thick leathery skin, vaguely humanoid face, and spider-like legs near it's head. It dwells in a frozen hollow beneath the Hearttree of Oldwood, where it writhes in it's madness.

The creature is capable of speech, but only spouts accusations, and vague threats towards the maddening sounds of a beating heart.

Hearttree of Oldwood

The Hearttree of Oldwood is a great ash tree growing on a large glade, reaching 20 feet above the other trees, and 40 feet wide in diameter. It's roots reach as high as 12 feet above ground, forming arches reaching from beneath the snow-covered ground. Due to this, the entire glade is a diffucult terrain.

There are multiple trails of passage marked with compressed snow and ice. A successful DC 12 Survival check is enough to establish that the creature (Frostroot) is covered in mucus, and had recently entered the hollow beneath the Hearttree.

There are two obvious solutions to this situation; either defeat Frostroot in open combat, or entrap him inside the hollow, and then burn him with the Hearttree.

If the characters decide to block the entrance to the hollow, have the participating characters succeed on a DC 15 Stealth check against Frostroots passive perception score. If the party fails this check, the angry spirit emerges from the hollow and attacks. If they succeed however, and trap unsuspecting Frostroot under the ash tree with a wooden barrier, it quickly freezes over, and the creature will be unable to get out when a fire is lit - in that case, describe:

As you ignite the Hearttree of Oldwood, flames hungrily engulf its ancient bark, spreading rapidly across its massive trunk. You hear the cackling of the air escaping its wood, and soon after, a terrible screech coming from the hollow beneath it. It lasts, for an uncofmortable moment, and slowly it wanes until the Hearttree is naught but a charred stump.

However, Frostroot can also be baited into the open with a loud noise. Whether the party prepares a trap, or intents to talk, the spirit emerges from the hollow, and engages - fighting to the death.

Ravenpass

Between the Oldwood and the town of Comairle there is a significant, and mostly impassable (due to recent snowfall) mountain range of Bearnach Peaks. The one exception is a route through the high mountains called Ravenpass.

The Hermit of the Pass

During their travel through the mountains, the adventurers might encounter a crazed old man in black burlap clothes, that approaches the travelers, and begins with a pitiful tyrade.

“Fools! Wanderers! What do you know of loss? Of the price paid for hubris? Oh, Morrigan, dark queen of ravens, what more must I endure? My goat—my only companion, my last tether to the world—gone! Stolen by fate, by shadows! Without it, I have nothing! No hope, no future, no life beyond this cursed rock!”

Fionnach the Lost, an odd hermit, laments the loss of his goat. Once a respected druid, he had disregarded a warning and demand for sacrifice from Morrigan. As punishment, the goddess of fate sent death upon his family and madness upon him, prophesying that he would spend half his life as a beggar, having only what he had refused to give her—his goat. The party crosses Ravenpass on the very day his time as a beggar equals the years of his life before the curse. While this does not end his punishment, it means that, if he were to die today, the prophecy would be fulfilled.

However, if the punishment continues, for every additional year he spends as a beggar, he hopes to live an equal number of years free, reclaiming a part of the life he lost.

Fionnach best the party to bring the goat to him, and as a reward, he promises to give the adventurers his lucky stone - a posession that he hid from the goddess herself. If the party agrees to help him, the hermit will tell them that he saw his goat climb up the mountain towards Raven's Perch.

Raven’s Perch

The Raven's Perch is a patch of black, oily stone that forms an overhang above the gorge of Ravenpass, named after the grim shadow it casts upon the pass.

When the party arrives, they can see the goat held by the cord by a beautiful woman - a Púca named Ryla. Amused by the suffering of Fionnach, she intends to kill the goat, which will cause the hermit to die;which she believes to be hilarious.

A successful DC 14 Persuasion check is enough for her to step down and give up the goat, on the condition that one of the characters agrees to grant her an undisclosed favor at a later time. If none of the characters agree, she pushes the goat down the perch, turns into a raven, and tries to escape.

If the characters manage to bring the goat back to Fionnach, he is overjoyed, but refuses to leave the vicinity of his cave. He makes good on his promise, and grants the party his Luckstone.

Town of Comairle

The town of Comairle is the traditional seat of the High King of Irgaelia, and a neutral ground open to all tribes, exempt from war and conflict. Its significance is tied to the Great Yew of Wisdom, the oldest tree in the realm, revered by druids. Temples and shrines to all divines dot the town, towering over the Alleys of the Fallen, the prestigious graveyard for esteemed heroes and wise figures.

The party arrives in Comairle with the task of delivering Dullahan's grim prophecy to the druids and the king, urging a council to convene and decide on the next steps. Having traveled from Cúilfeorna through the Ravenpass, they arrive at A13. Raven's Eye Gate.

A02. Grove of Concord

The Grove of Concord is a breathtaking expanse of nature's artistry, encircled by ancient oaks and towering yews whose sprawling branches form a near-canopy of emerald green. Shafts of golden sunlight pierce through the leaves, casting dappled patterns on the mossy ground below. In the heart of the grove lies a majestic stone circle, with monolithic stones weathered by time and adorned with carvings of swirling patterns and druidic symbols.

The Grove of Concord holds great significance as the site of the first druid gathering—Comairle, the town's namesake—and the venue for every subsequent meeting.

When the characters first arrive, the grove is tended by druid acolytes. Later, as the party progresses, the Archdruids of the East, West, and South gather here, awaiting the others.

##### The Comairle At the end of this chapter of the adventure, when all remaining Archdruids gather here, they begin their stargazing and debates (see Event: The Comairle).

A03. Yew of Wisdom

The Yew of Wisdom is the oldest and most revered tree in all of Irgaelia, towering over the heart of Comairle with its gnarled, ancient branches. Its deep crimson bark and dense evergreen foliage exude a timeless presence, inspiring awe in all who behold it. The tree radiates a subtle warmth that defies the chill of winter, creating a small oasis of green amidst the snow-covered town. This heat extends to the Grove of Concord.

Legends say the tree is a conduit for ancient wisdom, its roots intertwined with the spirits of the land. Visitors often leave offerings at its base, hoping to gain insight or blessings from the tree.

##### Offerings to the Yew

Offerings left at the Yew of Wisdom are typically gold, gems, or other treasures, placed in reverence by those seeking its blessings. However, those with keen insight should understand that such material gifts hold no true value to it. The Yew cares for what sustains it - water.

A character who waters the tree with pure, clean water will be rewarded. The Yew of Wisdom bestows upon them a Boon.

Boon of the Yew of Wisdom

The boon grants a permanent +1 bonus to your Wisdom ability score, up to a maximum of 24.

A04. Pool of Vows

Nestled at the edge of the sacred Grove of Concord, the Pool of Vows is one of the most revered sites in all of Irgaelia. The pool is traditionally used during important ceremonies, oaths, and rites of passage, for it is believed that those who swear vows or make promises here are bound by the strength of the ancient magic woven into the waters.

The Pool itself is a large, clear body of water, its surface smooth and undisturbed, save for the occasional ripple created by the soft winds that stir the branches above. The water is so clear that the stones at the bottom of the pool, some of which are said to have been placed by the gods themselves, are easily visible. The pool is surrounded by smooth, moss-covered stones that serve as seats for those who come to witness oaths or take part in the rituals.

A05. Arena of Honor

The Arena of Honor sits on the outskirts of Comairle, a grand circular amphitheater of rough-hewn stone, partially reclaimed by nature with ivy creeping up its walls. Towering yews and blackthorn trees line the perimeter, their branches forming a solemn canopy that shelters spectators. The air here is heavy with the scent of damp moss, leather, and the faint iron tang of past conflicts.

Built centuries ago by an alliance of warrior tribes, the Arena of Honor has served as a neutral ground to resolve disputes through skill and strength. Contests range from ritualized duels through displays of martial prowess to trials by combat, ensuring conflicts are settled without resorting to full-scale war. Although the arena is open to all tribes, each bout is overseen by impartial Heralds of the Balance, adjudicators appointed by the High King and clan chieftans to uphold fairness.

The arena floor is a vast, packed-dirt circle, encircled by a low stone barrier etched with runes of protection and balance. Four raised platforms mark the cardinal directions, each adorned with symbols representing the warrior spirits of the tribes: a wolf, a raven, a bear, and a serpent. Combatants may stand on these platforms to invoke blessings or call upon the spirits before battle.

A06. Drakescar Tower

Drakescar Tower is a tall, spiraling edifice of dark grey stone, rising like a jagged fang over the northern edge of Comairle. Named for the scar-like grooves carved into its surface — said to be remnants of an ancient battle with a dragon — the tower is the domain of Irgaelia’s most learned mages and scholars.

The tower's windows are narrow and tinted with amber and violet glass, casting strange, shifting lights on the cobbled paths around it. Though the interior is far larger than the exterior suggests (thanks to arcane expansions), the tower maintains a sense of containment and secrecy. Access to its upper floors is granted only to those with legitimate business, a proper invitation, or the wit to pass the trials of entry.

##### Key Features The Dragon’s Door :: The tower’s entrance is a reinforced oak door adorned with a bronze dragon-head knocker. It only opens when the knocker is used, followed by a correct riddle answer or a DC 16 Arcana check. The riddle changes daily, drawn from ancient draconic lore.

The Hall of Whispers :: The ground floor is a circular chamber lined with shelves of arcane tomes, illuminated by hovering orbs of soft blue light. Whispering voices emanate from the walls, an ambient chorus of spells being practiced and refined. Visitors can peruse the shelves, but the

The Observatory :: On clear nights, the tower’s uppermost level opens to reveal a domed ceiling of transparent crystal. This space is used for stargazing, divination, and celestial studies. Visitors might encounter mages charting the stars or consulting astral charts for visions of the future.

A08. Alleys of the Fallen

The Alleys of the Fallen form a winding, labyrinthine network of narrow streets and shadowed passages that weave through the oldest part of Comairle. This district serves as a graveyard and memorial to fallen heroes, wise figures, and legendary champions of Irgaelia. In fact, one of the greatest honors a hero or scholar may achieve is to earn himself a column here, for their ashes to be kept, adorned with a whispering plaque.

##### Key Features

The Lantern Walk :: A narrow, cobblestone street lined with wrought-iron lanterns, that crosses straight through the graveyard from it's south to north edge. A22. Gate of Morrigan

At the top of the far western side of Temple Hill, the Gate of Morrigan is said to be the threshold to Morrigan’s dominion. Two withered trees stand sentinel over the pool, their gnarled limbs intertwining above like skeletal fingers forming an arch. The path ends at a heavy stone door, with a weathered and cracked surface. The door bears an intricate, faintly glowing sigil.

1. A New Challenger

As the party prepares to meet with the Archdruids or the king, a local hero challenges one of them to a contest, questioning their worthiness for such a critical task. This challenge could either be physical (such as a duel with weapons) or intellectual (like a riddle contest or trial of wit).

2. The Weeping Statue

A crowd gathers around a statue of a Brigid in one of Comairle's bustling squares. The statue appears to be weeping tears of blood. The townsfolk are divided—some see it as an omen, others suspect a trick.

3. A Runaway Cart

A horse-drawn cart laden with barrels of mead careens wildly down a steep street, threatening to crash into a vendor’s stall. The driver shouts for help, unable to control the panicked beast.

4. A Scuffle at the Market

A heated argument erupts between a noble and a street vendor, drawing a crowd. Accusations of theft fly, and it looks like the situation may turn violent.

5. The Vanishing Minstrel

A street performer strumming a lute suddenly disappears in a puff of smoke, leaving behind a scattering of coins.

6. A Merchant’s Plight

A merchant’s cart has overturned in a narrow street, spilling rare silks and spices everywhere.

7. The Reluctant Courier

A breathless courier rushes up to the party and thrusts a sealed letter into one of their hands, babbling about missing their delivery and fleeing before they can ask questions. The letter is addressed to someone influential in the city.

8. A Thief in the Shadows

A nimble pickpocket makes off with one of the party’s coin purses, darting into a maze of narrow alleys. Pursuit reveals the thief is a desperate child trying to feed their family.

9. The Singing Well

The party passes an old well in the square, where locals claim to hear faint singing when coins are dropped. It is a clever con by an entertainer looking to draw tips.

10. A Street Prophet

A wild-eyed prophet, clad in rags and clutching a broken staff, blocks the party’s path, proclaiming cryptic warnings of doom.

11. The Merchant Duel

Two rival merchants stand nose-to-nose in the street, shouting accusations of sabotage, a spilled barrel of fish guts between them. Both ask the party to mediate before the city watch intervenes.

12. The Cloaked Figure

A cloaked figure loiters near the Silver Chalice, watching the party.

13. Lost and Found

A child runs up to the party, unable to find their parents. A suspicious group is watching, intent on taking the child for ransom.

14. A Broken Sign

A wooden shop sign hangs precariously above the street, threatening to fall. The shopkeeper rushes out, pleading for help to secure it before someone is hurt.

15. The Overzealous Watchman

A city watchman, eager to impress their superiors, accuses the party of a minor infraction — real or imagined.

16. The Wandering Healer

A wandering healer stops the party and offers a blessing in exchange for a small donation.

17. A Riddle Contest

A group of townsfolk surrounds a flamboyant entertainer who challenges all comers to answer their riddles.

18. A Street Brawl

A drunken brawl spills out from a tavern into the cobblestone streets.

19. The Cry of the Gull

A strange gull circles overhead, cawing incessantly and dropping small trinkets near the party.

20. A Broken Fountain

Water from a central fountain flows erratically, splashing into the street and drenching passersby.

Tower of the Blind Eye

An old and abandoned watchtower ten miles west from Comairle had been repurposed for a base of a cabaal of wizards known as the Watchers of the Blind Eye. The characters are most likely to find this place guided by Siobhan Blackfeather, the bogborn high priestess of Morrigan, in search of the Raven's Shard.

Cabal of the Blind Eye

The cabal consists of five wizards united in their defiance of Morrigan, the Phantom Queen. They believe the goddess has too much power over mortal fate and seek to sever her influence by unlocking the secrets of prophecy and destiny. With The Raven’s Shard in their possession, they are working to amplify their divination and protective magics to shield themselves from Morrigan's gaze.

The group is responsible for stealing The Raven's Shard and will not give it up under any circumstances.

Members of the Cabal

Áedán (CN Human Mage of the Blind Eye) :: Old Evoker with a tangled beard.

Saoirse (NE Half-Elf Mage of the Blind Eye) :: Pallid, with an aura of cold detachment.

Cairbre (LE Tiefling Mage of the Blind Eye) :: A methodical but cowardly abjurer.

Brígh (N Elf Mage of the Blind Eye) :: An enigmatic conjurer, a negotiator at heart.

Tómas (CN Dwarf Mage of the Blind Eye) :: An illusionist with a booming laugh.

Ruined Watchtower

The Tower of the Blind Eye stands as a crumbling sentinel on a windswept hill near the town of Comairle. Once a guardian's watchpost, it has fallen into disrepair, its stone walls cracked, and its battlements partially collapsed. Now, it serves as the lair of the Watchers of the Blind Eye.

The structure had been overgrown by several thick trees growing around and through it. \column

B1. Approach

The path leading to the tower is overgrown with thorny brambles and flanked by gnarled trees. Strange carvings depicting a single, weeping eye mark the path at irregular intervals. These carvings are a physical mark of an Alarm spell that wil send a ping to one of the members of the cabal. The characters may choose to climb up by succeding a DC 10 Atlethics check, but to do so unnoticed requires muffling the climbing sounds.

B2. Main Entrance

The main entrance to the watchtower is protected by a rusted, iron-bound door. It stands slightly ajar, allowing the characters to slip through. Members of the cabal close these doors at night. Although there is no lock, they make a squeeky noise loud enough to awaken them.

B3. First Floor

The first floor of the tower serves as a common sleeping area. Crude beds are pushed against stone walls, between colossal roots that burst through the stone like through a gaping wound.

If the characters managed to sneak past the alarm spells during the day, Saoirse and Brígh are here. Although not immediatelly hostile, they are unwilling to give up the Raven's Shard. A successful DC 15 Intimidation or Persuasion check encourages them to give up a fake crystal (DC 15 Insight or Arcana check required to realise the ruse). If violence breaks out, the mages from second floor (B4) join the fight in one round.

During the night, all of the mebers of the cabal sleep there, not keeping watch, instead depending on the alarm spells, and the protection of the heavy doors.

B4. Second Floor

The second floor contains remnants of old guard quarters repurposed as a crude laboratory. Shelves hold alchemical tools, dusty tomes, and fragments of crystals, with the Raven Shard in Áedán's posession. The remaining memebers of the cabal are in this room.
